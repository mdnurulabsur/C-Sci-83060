The art biennale began as a single exhibition in Venice in 1895. For nearly a century it grew slowly — only 36 biennales existed worldwide by 1990. Then everything changed. Driven by globalization and a rapidly expanding art world, the number exploded. In the 1990s alone the total nearly tripled, growing by 197%. By 2018, 200 active biennales were spread across 81 cities on every inhabited continent. The map below plots each one, colored by the era in which it was founded.

01 — Main Visualization  ·  World Map
Where Biennales Were Born: A Geography of Founding Eras
Each dot = one biennale at its founding city. Color shows the founding era. Notice how the early decades were almost entirely European, while Asia and the Global South surge strongly after 2000.
02 — Supporting Visualization A
Exponential Growth, 1895–2018
Total number of active biennales in existence each year. The curve is nearly flat for 90 years, then turns sharply upward after 1990. The 2008 financial crisis caused a brief pause — 2011 saw just one new biennale founded — but growth resumed quickly and reached 200 by 2018.
02 — Supporting Visualization B
Biennales by World Region, 2018
Western Europe leads with 57 biennales, but Asia's rise to second place with 41 is the defining story of the past two decades. The geography of biennales does not match the art market — Moscow, Riga, and Taipei each host more biennales than most global art capitals.

Dataset: 200 art biennales active in 2017–2018, compiled by Alise Tifentalle (Fall 2018). Analysis: Lev Manovich, "Examining the Growth of Art Biennales from 1895 to 2019," Cultural Analytics Lab, 2021. Three biennales with missing geographic coordinates are omitted from the map.
